# IELTS / TOEFL speaking scoring reference

Updated 2026-09-18 after checking official IELTS and ETS materials. This file is the product reference for prompts and report labels; it is not a replacement for examiner training or an official score conversion.

## IELTS Speaking

The official IELTS Speaking Band Descriptors for Academic and General Training use four equally weighted criteria:

- Fluency and Coherence: keep going, organize ideas, connect points and develop answers.
- Lexical Resource: range, precision, appropriacy and paraphrasing.
- Grammatical Range and Accuracy: variety of structures and how often errors affect meaning.
- Pronunciation: intelligibility, individual sounds, word stress, connected speech, rhythm and intonation.

The report uses half-band practice estimates from 1–9 and should assess the average quality of the response sample. The product should prompt the learner to answer directly, extend with a reason/example and keep development coherent. A single short conversation cannot establish an official IELTS band.

Official references: [IELTS Speaking Band Descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf), [IELTS resources for setting scores](https://ielts.org/organisations/ielts-for-organisations/understanding-ielts-scoring/resources-for-setting-your-ielts-scores), and [IELTS explanation of Bands 6 and 7](https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/differences-between-ielts-6-and-7-for-productive-skills).

## TOEFL iBT Speaking

The TOEFL iBT changed score reporting on January 21, 2026. The current section score is 1–6 in half-point increments, aligned with CEFR. During a two-year transition, reports may also include a comparable 0–120 total. The app therefore displays the current 1–6 Speaking practice estimate as primary. Older saved reports without the new framework are marked as legacy 0–30 values in history.

The current Speaking section uses two task types:

- Listen and Repeat: process short spoken sentences and repeat them accurately and intelligibly.
- Take an Interview: answer academic or campus-life interview questions while maintaining natural pace and using appropriate grammar and vocabulary.

For learner-facing feedback, the app groups the observable priorities into delivery/intelligibility, language use (vocabulary and grammar), and topic development. At the performance level, official ETS descriptors emphasize clarity, pace, intelligibility, grammatical/vocabulary control, organization, completeness and support. The app's 1–6 result is an exam-oriented practice estimate; it does not reproduce ETS's combination of AI scoring and certified raters.

Official references: [ETS TOEFL iBT Speaking section](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/about/content/speaking.html), [ETS score-scale update](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/scores/understand-scores.html), [TOEFL iBT Performance Descriptors](https://www.ets.org/pdfs/toefl/toefl-ibt-performance-descriptors.pdf), and [TOEFL Speaking Scoring Guide](https://www.ets.org/pdfs/toefl/speaking-rubrics.pdf).

## Product scoring policy

The app labels every IELTS/TOEFL result as a practice estimate. It keeps the four IELTS criteria visible and maps TOEFL feedback to the current 1–6 section scale. It does not infer a score from facial expression, eye position or gesture. The expression-analysis feature remains descriptive and context-dependent; exam scoring uses speech content and conversational/task evidence.

The fallback report uses deterministic signals such as response length, connectors, topic words and available session structure. Those signals are coaching heuristics, not validated IELTS or TOEFL scoring. A real exam prediction requires a sufficiently long, task-matched response and should be validated against expert ratings.
